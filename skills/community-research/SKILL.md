# Research how people actually use it

## Align before broad research

Before broad research, briefly restate your understanding of the user's desired
outcome, relevant constraints and proposed scope. Ask the user to confirm or
correct it, and wait for alignment before broad searches or research delegation.
Carry forward explicit alignment already established for the same task; revisit
it when the goal or scope materially changes.

Exploring new uses can yield several useful possibilities; choosing a solution
requires an assessment against the user's current setup and actual workflow.

For an existing setup, identify the unmet need and research the relevant practice
or choice. Keep working parts outside the comparison unless they affect that need.

## Find cases and establish what they show

Search where the practice lives: upstream issues and discussions, plugin or
package repositories, practitioner write-ups and relevant communities. Choose
sources for the question rather than working through a fixed platform checklist.

Follow promising leads to the original case. Identify the problem, setup, method,
observed result and tradeoff that make it informative. Include limitations,
abandoned approaches or disagreement when they could change the recommendation.
Repeated retellings of one case are still one case.
For comparative claims, check whether differences in workload, environment or
access path could explain the reported results.

Use firsthand accounts to understand experience. Check technical capabilities,
defaults and current support against upstream documentation, code or maintainers.
Distinguish a supported option from a recommendation, and a demo or author's
claim from independent use. Date and version matter when judging whether an old
workaround or complaint still applies. Limited evidence supports a limited claim.

## Relate the findings to the user's work

Check whether native features or the user's existing tools cover the need before
adding tools or devising a custom workaround. Explain what the option changes in
practice, the conditions needed, and its operational or maintenance burden. Keep
your adaptation of a case distinct from what its original author demonstrated.

Before presenting a setting or workflow as a new next step, check relevant
available configuration or prior decisions to see whether it is already in use.
If that state is unavailable, make the recommendation conditional.

Present the useful patterns or choices with source links and applicability,
including uncertainty that affects the decision. Where a small trial would resolve
a meaningful uncertainty, describe what it should distinguish; running it follows
the current task's authorization. Retaining the current setup is a valid outcome.

Deliver in the conversation unless the user requests a saved artifact or the
task's established deliverable requires one.
